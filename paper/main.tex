\title{Calibration of extrapolated logical error rates: nominal 95\% intervals of constant-prefactor fits showed low empirical coverage}

\author{Adam Faust}
\affiliation{MOBLUEHQ, Inc., Long Grove, Illinois, USA}
\date{\ifdraftmark DRAFT v8, not for distribution. \fi November 2026.}

\begin{abstract}
Resource estimates for fault-tolerant quantum computers rest on logical error rates $\pL$ too small to simulate; they come from extrapolating fits made at high physical error rate $p$. We measure how often the 95\% intervals of such fits contain reference estimates obtained independently by importance sampling stratified by the number of faults, for a rotated surface-code memory at $d=3,5,7$ under circuit-level depolarising noise with matching decoding, at $p=10^{-3}$ to $10^{-6}$, with \V{reps} replicates and two fit windows. Fits with the exponent fixed at $(d+1)/2$ and a constant prefactor have median predictions within \V{ratio.fixed.lowpct.max}\% of the reference on the first window ($p=4\times10^{-3}$ to $1.5\times10^{-2}$) and \V{bw.fixed.maxdev}\% on the second ($1.5\times10^{-3}$ to $4\times10^{-3}$), but their upper 95\% limits are only \V{hw.R3.min}--\V{bw.hw.R2.max}\% above the prediction (R2, a constant per distance: \V{hw.R2.max}--\V{bw.hw.R2.max}\%; R3, one shared curve: \V{hw.R3.min}--\V{hw.R3.max}\%). Coverage at the reference point estimates is below 90\% in \V{v6.fx.w1.pt} of \V{bw4.cells} cells on the first window and \V{v6.fx.w2.pt} of \V{bw4.cells} on the second; observed hit fractions stayed below 90\% throughout the stated reference ranges in \V{v6.fx.w1.robust} and \V{v6.fx.w2.robust} cells, and in none at or above it. These ranges do not cover a bias shared by the reference estimates or the assumed zero-failure strata. A free-exponent power law was high by up to \V{ratio.R1.d7.t6}-fold on the first window and covered \V{bw.R1.covmin}--\V{bw.R1.covmax}\% on the second, contrary to our prediction; the windows differ in span and distance as well as position. A form with the same leading power and a smooth prefactor covered \V{am.R4p.lowd.covmin}--\V{am.R4p.lowd.covmax}\% at $d=3$ and $5$ on the first window (post hoc); on the second it missed its registered bar, three of eight hit fractions exceeding the prespecified 97\% cutoff. At $d=7$ several verdicts are undecided (reference errors \V{truthse.d7.far.min}--\V{truthse.d7.far.max}\% at $p\le10^{-4}$). One code, noise model and decoder; no published estimate examined.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A logical qubit is useful when its error rate $\pL$ is far below the physical error rate $p$ of its parts. For the surface code \cite{dennis2002,fowler2012} the rates that matter for applications, $10^{-10}$ and below, cannot be reached by direct Monte Carlo simulation, which needs of order $1/\pL$ shots. The usual practice is to simulate where failures are frequent, fit a formula, and extrapolate. The formula is often a power law in $p$ with exponent $(d+1)/2$ for odd code distance $d$ \cite{fowler2012,litinski2019}, sometimes with a free exponent, sometimes with a correction factor \cite{bravyi2024}; in experiments the error suppression factor $\Lambda$ between successive distances is projected to larger codes \cite{google2023,google2025}. Resource estimates such as Ref.~\cite{gidneyekera2021} build on numbers of this kind, so a quoted uncertainty should be known to be reliable.

The question here is narrow: when a fit of one of these forms reports a 95\% interval for $\pL$ at a much lower $p$, how often does that interval contain the right value? It can be answered only where $\pL$ is known independently of the fit. We obtained independent reference estimates for the real circuit at small distances and scored the fits against them, on two fit windows.

\emph{Related work and what is claimed.} Reaching the rare-event regime without a fit in $p$ is an active subject: splitting \cite{bravyivargo2013,mayer2025}, sampling subsets of faults of fixed size \cite{heussen2024,ye2026}, the failure spectrum \cite{beverland2025}, and enumeration of a detector error model in order of weight with sound bounds \cite{molavi2026}. Our reference estimator is our own implementation of these ideas, independent and not compared with them; \SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:cert} combines a proposal-specific score bound with the unsampled-mass bound of Ref.~\cite{heussen2024}. Fits to Monte Carlo data are known to extrapolate badly: Appendix~A.3 of Ref.~\cite{beverland2025} reports it for the form $p^{d/2}\exp(a+bp+cp^2)$ on a code of even distance, and Ref.~\cite{mullan2026} shows that a decoder which mishandles one hook error can make a convincing high-$p$ fit underestimate the failure rate at low $p$. What we add is a measurement of a different quantity: the repeated-sampling coverage of the nominal intervals of conventional fits in $p$, against independently estimated circuit rates. In the parts of these works we read, none reports such a coverage frequency. No systematic survey has been made, and we do not claim priority.

\section{Design}\label{sec:design}
\subsection{Circuit, decoder and units}\label{sec:units}
The circuit is the rotated surface-code memory experiment in the $Z$ basis generated by Stim \cite{stim2021}, with $d$ rounds at distance $d$. The noise is uniform circuit-level depolarising: a depolarising channel of strength $p$ after every Clifford gate, and a flip with probability $p$ before each measurement and after each reset. Decoding is minimum-weight perfect matching with PyMatching \cite{sparseblossom2025}, with edge weights taken from the circuit's detector error model at the same $p$.

$\pL$ is always the \emph{block} rate, the probability of a logical error per memory experiment of $d$ rounds; every fit, target, reference estimate and coverage number is a block rate. Rates at different distances are compared \emph{per round},
\begin{equation}
\epsilon(d)=\tfrac12\bigl[1-(1-2\pL)^{1/d}\bigr]\approx\pL/d ,\label{eq:perround}
\end{equation}
as is conventional \cite{google2023,google2025}, and $\Lambda=\epsilon(d)/\epsilon(d+2)$.

\subsection{Fit data and recipes}\label{sec:recipes}
Each window has five points, each simulated until at least 300 logical errors have been seen, with \V{reps} independent replicate datasets at each of $d=3,5,7$ (\V{windowpoints} points per window) and seeds fixed in advance. The \emph{first window} is $p\in\{4,6,8,10,15\}\times10^{-3}$; it straddles a finite-size crossing of the curves (Sec.~\ref{sec:fig1}). The \emph{second window} was added by a registered amendment after the results on the first were known. Its position was set by a rule that uses neither the new data nor any reference estimate: the crossing $p_\times=\V{bw.px}\times10^{-3}$ of the mean logical error rates of $d=3$ and $d=7$ on the first window, times $\{0.18,0.24,0.30,0.36,0.48\}$, rounded to the nearest $0.5\times10^{-3}$, which gives $p\in\{1.5,2,2.5,3,4\}\times10^{-3}$. The two windows differ in three ways at once: the second lies below the crossing; it is narrower (its ends differ by a factor of \V{bw.span.new}, against \V{bw.span.old}), which by itself widens the interval of any fit with a free slope; and it is \V{bw.gapdiff} decades nearer the targets. The design cannot assign a difference between the windows to any one of these.

Each recipe (Table~\ref{tab:recipes}) is fitted to each replicate by weighted least squares on $\ln\hat\pL$, where $\hat\pL=k/n$ for $k$ failures in $n$ shots and the weight of a point is $k/(1-\hat\pL)$, the inverse delta-method variance estimate of $\ln\hat\pL$. R1, R2 and R4$'$ are fitted to each distance separately; R3 is fitted to all three at once, with its scale $p_0$ a fitted constant. R5 takes the R1 fits at $d=3$ and $5$ and applies the suppression between them once more, to project $d=7$. As registered, R5 applies that step to block rates, which was an error of ours: if the per-round rate is geometric in $d$, then by Eq.~(\ref{eq:perround}) the block-rate projection $\pL(5)^2/\pL(3)$ exceeds $\pL(7)$ by the factor $\V{v6.alg}=\V{v6.algval}$ in the small-rate limit (the exact conversion is Eq.~(\ref{eq:perround}) and its inverse). R5 is scored as registered, and a per-round version is reported beside it as post hoc. Each recipe reports the 95\% interval it would naturally report, the delta-method interval $\hat\mu\pm1.96\,\hat\sigma$ for $\ln\pL$ at the target, from the binomial errors as they stand. The interval for $\pL$ is the exponential of this, $[e^{\hat\mu-1.96\hat\sigma},\,e^{\hat\mu+1.96\hat\sigma}]$, symmetric on the logarithmic scale. The \emph{half-width} quoted throughout is the upper end relative to the prediction $e^{\hat\mu}$, that is $e^{1.96\hat\sigma}-1$: a half-width of \V{bw.hw.R1.t6.max}\%, the median for R1 on the second window at $p=10^{-6}$, puts the lower limit \V{v7.sc.lower}\% below the prediction and the upper \V{bw.hw.R1.t6.max}\% above. A sensitivity analysis that multiplies the covariance by $\max(1,\chi^2/\mathrm{dof})$ is in the \SM. These are our implementations of common forms, not a reconstruction of the procedure of any particular paper.

\subsection{Metric and reference range}\label{sec:metric}
The targets are $p=10^{-3},10^{-4},10^{-5},10^{-6}$. A replicate \emph{hits} if the reference estimate of $\pL$ lies in its interval. Coverage is the fraction of the \V{reps} replicates that hit, with a Clopper--Pearson 95\% interval \cite{clopperpearson1934}; the hit count and exact interval of every cell are in \SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:covrange} (285 of 300 is 91.9--97.2\%; the cells are not independent). A cell (recipe, $d$, target) is \emph{miscalibrated} if coverage is below 90\% and \emph{calibrated} if it lies in 93--97\%. These are screening rules, and the four targets of a cell row reuse each replicate's fit.

The registered test treats the reference as exact. Beside every coverage number we therefore give its \emph{reference range}: the smallest and largest hit fraction as one shared reference value moves over the estimate $\pm1.96$ standard errors. A cell is \emph{robust-below} if the largest hit count in its range is under 270 of 300 (90\%), \emph{robust-above} if the smallest is 270 or more, and \emph{undecided} otherwise; every such label in this paper is generated from the integer hit counts by this one rule. The range is post hoc.

\subsection{Reference estimates}\label{sec:ref}
The values against which the fits are scored are estimates, not exact values. They come from importance sampling stratified by the number of faults, an established approach \cite{heussen2024,beverland2025,ye2026,molavi2026}; the sampling law, the working standard error and all constants of our implementation are in \SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:method}. Stim's detector error model of the circuit at the target $p$ is a list of $N$ independent fault mechanisms ($N=\V{m.N.d3}$, \V{m.N.d5} and \V{m.N.d7} at $d=3,5,7$). With $W$ the number of mechanisms that occur,
\begin{equation}
\pL=\sum_{w\ge1}P(W=w)\,f_w ,\label{eq:strata}
\end{equation}
where $f_w$ is the probability of failure given exactly $w$ faults. $P(W=w)$ is computed exactly from elementary symmetric polynomials. Each $f_w$, for $w=1$ to $120$, is estimated by drawing fault sets from a mixture of a local-growth proposal, which favours the clustered faults that defeat a matching decoder, and draws without replacement in proportion to the odds of each mechanism, which reach every set and keep the weights bounded; each set is decoded with the decoder of Sec.~\ref{sec:units}. The probability of more than 120 faults is below $\V{v6.tail.d7}$ in every reference cell.

Sampling is adaptive: the strata that contribute most to a working standard error $\hat\sigma$ receive further draws until $\hat\sigma$ is at most 2\% of the estimate, or a limit of 2400~s or $4\times10^7$ draws is reached. Because allocation and stopping depend on the draws already made, the estimate is not exactly unbiased and $\hat\sigma$ is not a guaranteed bound. Strata with $w<(d+1)/2$, in which none of $10^5$ draws failed in any run, are set to zero, which for the circuit is an assumption. Each of the twelve reference cells ($d\in\{3,5,7\}$ by four targets) combines two independent runs, weighted by $1/\hat\sigma^2$. All \V{m.lowd.runs} runs at $d=3$ and $5$ stopped at the 2\% rule. All \V{m4.d7.ref.runs} runs at $d=7$ reached the time limit first, with $\hat\sigma$ between \V{m.runrelse.d7.min}\% and \V{m.runrelse.d7.max}\% of the estimate, and were not extended.

A second check uses a \emph{linearised surrogate} of the detector error model, in which each mechanism has probability proportional to $p$ and the decoder weights are frozen at their $p=10^{-3}$ values. For the surrogate, $\pL$ is a polynomial in $p$ whose coefficients through fourth order were computed exactly by decoding every set of up to four faults at $d=3$ and $5$ (\SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:shells}). The surrogate is not the reference for the coverage tables.

\subsection{Registration and standing of the analyses}\label{sec:when}
Analyses marked registered were committed to the author's private repository before the quantity was computed. \SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:status} summarizes the main registered comparisons; additional registered checks and their outcomes appear in \SM\ Secs.~S1--S4 and S7. The original plan was committed before any coverage was computed but after the window data and the first reference run of each cell had been generated and the checks of the reference estimator seen. Our records show no comparison of a prediction with a reference estimate before then; we cannot show that the window data were never looked at.

Figure~\ref{fig:money} shows the first window. The mean logical error rates reverse their order in $d$ inside it (\SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:window}); the pairwise crossings of the window means are at \V{v6.cross.35}, \V{v6.cross.37} and $\V{v6.cross.57}\times10^{-3}$ for $d=3/5$, $3/7$ and $5/7$. We call this a finite-size crossing and do not identify it with the threshold.

\looseness=-1 The panels show what every recipe has to contend with. Write $\pL=p^{h}A(p)$ with $h=(d+1)/2$. At the sampled low-$p$ targets the reference prefactor is nearly constant for $d=3$, $5$ and dips by about 15\% at $d=7$ at the two middle targets; the window data show it declining at all three distances, so the local slope of $\ln\pL$ against $\ln p$ is $h+d\ln A/d\ln p<h$. A constant prefactor (R2, R3) averages over the fall and lands below the plateau. A free exponent (R1) reads the fall as a shallower slope and extrapolates high. Both propagate the estimated binomial sampling errors of the window points through the fit; the primary intervals have no term for prefactor drift. A smooth prefactor with the right leading power (R4$'$) can follow the fall and return to the plateau. In the surrogate the next-to-leading coefficient of the polynomial is negative at $d=3$ and $5$, consistent with such a fall (\SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:shells}).

\subsection{Checks on the reference estimates}\label{sec:truthcheck}
\looseness=-1 Against direct Monte Carlo on the real circuit, the estimator agreed within $|z|\le2$ in \V{val.ok} of \V{val.eligible} eligible cells (largest $|z|=\V{val.zmax}$; registered bar 90\% of cells). Direct validation stops at $\pL\approx\V{val.pmin}$, while the reference estimates extend to $\V{truth.d7.t6}$; below the validated range they rest on the estimator's own error analysis and on the surrogate. Two separate checks use the surrogate's exact fourth-order polynomial, which is a truncation: the working estimator's interval contained it in \V{v6.br.is.touch} of \V{v6.br.is.n} seeds (registered bar: at least 18 of 20 per cell; \SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:method}), and the separate betting intervals of \SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:cert} contained it in \V{c.inside.all} of \V{c.n.all} replicates (registered bar: all). A bracket for the full surrogate value is usable only at $d=3$ (\SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:bracket}).

\looseness=-1 For the twelve reference cells the two runs agree (largest $|z|=\V{truth.zmax}$, at $d=3$, $p=10^{-4}$; at most \V{v6.run.zmax} at $d=7$; all twelve are in \SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:truth}). The combined relative standard error is \V{truthse.lowd.min}--\V{truthse.lowd.max}\% at $d=3$ and $5$, \V{truthse.d7.t3}\% at $d=7$, $p=10^{-3}$, and \V{truthse.d7.far.min}--\V{truthse.d7.far.max}\% at $d=7$ for the three lower targets. Divided by $p^4$, the $d=7$ references are \V{v6.d7c.ivw.t3}, \V{v6.d7c.ivw.t4}, \V{v6.d7c.ivw.t5} and $\V{v6.d7c.ivw.t6}\times10^{6}$ at $p=10^{-3}$ to $10^{-6}$: a dip of about 15\% at the two middle targets. The dip is not produced by the weighting (equal weights move the combined values by at most \V{v6.eq.maxdiff}\%; \SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:refs}). A downward bias of about 10\% at the middle targets, from heavy fault sets missed by both runs, is neither shown nor excluded.

This matters for the verdicts. The R2 intervals at $d=7$ are $\pm$\V{hw.R2.max}\% wide and the admissible references (\SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:r2d7}) differ by more: the first-window hit fraction of R2 at $p=10^{-4}$ is \V{v6.r2d7.w1.min.t4}\% against one run and \V{v6.r2d7.w1.max.t4}\% against the other. The first-window verdicts for R2 and R3 at $d=7$ are therefore undecided at $p=10^{-4}$ and $10^{-5}$ (R3 also at $10^{-6}$), and we draw no conclusion from those cells.

At $d=3$ and $5$ it is zero in all sixteen cells and at most \V{am.ru.fixed.lowd.hitmax}\% for any reference value in the range; the median error there is \V{fixed.sigmas.min} to \V{fixed.sigmas.max} of the fits' own standard errors. Over the reference range R2 is robustly below 90\% in \V{v7.w1.R2.all.below} of its 12 cells and R3 in \V{v7.w1.R3.all.below} of 12; the \V{v7.w1.R2.all.undecided} and \V{v7.w1.R3.all.undecided} undecided cells are all at $d=7$.

\looseness=-1 On the second window the median predictions span \V{bw.ratio.fixed.min}--\V{bw.ratio.fixed.max} times the reference. They stay below it at every target for R2 at $d=3,5$ and R3 at $d=5$. The upper limits are again close to the prediction, \V{bw.hw.R2.max}\% (R2) and \V{bw.hw.R3.min}--\V{bw.hw.R3.max}\% (R3) above it, and a bias of at most \V{bw.fixed.maxdev}\% is enough to defeat them. Coverage is below 90\% in \V{v6.fx.w2.pt} of 24 cells, where the registered prediction BW-4 needed 16. The bias is now comparable with the uncertainty of the reference, and R2 and R3 must be read separately. R2, the fixed-exponent fit of each distance on its own, is below 90\% at the point estimates in \V{v7.w2.R2.all.pt} of its 12 cells but robustly so in \V{v7.w2.R2.all.below} (\V{v7.w2.R2.lowd.below} of 8 at $d=3,5$ and \V{v7.w2.R2.d7.below} of 4 at $d=7$); \V{v7.w2.R2.all.undecided} are undecided. R3, whose single curve is forced through three distances, is below 90\% at \V{v7.w2.R3.all.pt} of 12 and robustly in \V{v7.w2.R3.all.below} (\V{v7.w2.R3.lowd.below} of 8 at $d=3,5$, all at $d=5$, where it predicts \V{bw.ratio.R3.d5.t4}--\V{bw.ratio.R3.d5.t3} of the reference against \V{bw.ratio.R2.d5.t4}--\V{bw.ratio.R2.d5.t3} for R2; and \V{v7.w2.R3.d7.below} of 4 at $d=7$); \V{v7.w2.R3.all.undecided} are undecided. The pooled \V{v6.fx.w2.pt} of 24 below 90\% at the point estimates therefore mixes the effect of a constant prefactor (R2) with a further bias from the common curve (R3). In neither window does any cell of R2 or R3 stay at or above 90\% over the whole range. Scaling the covariance by $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ changes little (\V{bw4.sca.mis} of \V{bw4.cells} below 90\%; \SM\ Table~\ref{S-tab:bwsca}).

On the first window, prefactor drift explains the direction of the R2 misses; the interval has no term for it. Two registered repairs of these intervals, adding a discrepancy variance estimated from a held-out window point and dividing out the zero-fault factor, both failed their bars (\SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:inflate}).

\subsection{The free-exponent fit and the fit window}\label{sec:bw}
On the first window R1 over-predicts, and the factor grows with each decade: at $d=7$ the median prediction is \V{ratio.R1.d7.t3}, \V{ratio.R1.d7.t4}, \V{ratio.R1.d7.t5} and \V{ratio.R1.d7.t6} times the reference at $p=10^{-3}$ to $10^{-6}$. Its 95\% half-widths are \V{hw.R1.min}--\V{hw.R1.max}\% of the prediction, wide but not wide enough for errors of that size. Coverage is at most \V{cov.pri.max}\%, and all \V{v6.R1.w1.robust} verdicts are robust.

\looseness=-1 On the second window we had predicted that severe undercoverage would persist (BW-1), and it did not, while the prediction that R1 would improve (BW-2) held. R1's hit fraction at the reference point estimate is between \V{bw.R1.covmin}\% and \V{bw.R1.covmax}\% in all twelve cells, all above 90\% (\SM\ Fig.~\ref{S-fig:win}), with a median of \V{bw.R1.median}\%, and its median prediction is \V{bw.ratio.R1.min}--\V{bw.ratio.R1.max} times the reference. Over the reference range \V{v6.R1.w2.above} of the 12 cells stay at or above 90\% and \V{v6.R1.w2.strad} are undecided. The higher hit fractions coincided with closer point predictions and wide intervals at the lowest targets: the median 95\% half-width is \V{bw.hw.R1.t3.max}\% of the prediction at $p=10^{-3}$, \V{bw.hw.R1.t4.max}\% at $10^{-4}$, \V{bw.hw.R1.t5.max}\% at $10^{-5}$ and \V{bw.hw.R1.t6.max}\% at $10^{-6}$, where the upper limit is \V{v7.sc.ul} times the lower. It is also a little short of nominal: \V{bw3.inband.R1} of the 12 cells are in the 93--97\% band and none reaches 95\%.

The median fitted exponents are \V{slope.d3}, \V{slope.d5} and \V{slope.d7} on the first window and \V{bw.slope.d3}, \V{bw.slope.d5} and \V{bw.slope.d7} on the second, against \V{nominal.d3}, \V{nominal.d5} and \V{nominal.d7} for $(d+1)/2$. R1 is fitted to each distance separately and has no information about where two distances cross; what it responds to is the local slope deficit $d\ln A/d\ln p$ across its window (Sec.~\ref{sec:fig1}). We read its miss as an effect of prefactor curvature and the crossing as a heuristic marker of where that is large. The run establishes only that on a window chosen by a fixed rule to lie below the crossing the registered failure of R1 did not occur.

\subsection{The leading power with a smooth prefactor}\label{sec:exp}
R4$'$ is the form $p^{(d+1)/2}\exp(c_0+c_1p+c_2p^2)$. On the first window, where it is post hoc, it covered the reference in \V{am.R4p.lowd.covmin}--\V{am.R4p.lowd.covmax}\% of replicates in all eight cells at $d=3$ and $5$, with median predictions within \V{am.R4p.lowd.ratmin}--\V{am.R4p.lowd.ratmax} of the reference and reference ranges of \V{v6.R4p.w1.lowd.rgmin}--\V{v6.R4p.w1.lowd.rgmax}\%.

\looseness=-1 On the second window R4$'$ was run on the stored data under a registered bar: coverage in 93--97\% in at least \V{r4b.bar} of the \V{r4b.cells} cells at $d=3$ and $5$. \emph{The bar was not met}: \V{r4b.inband} of \V{r4b.cells} cells are in the band. The \V{r4b.above} misses are all at $d=3$, at $p\le10^{-4}$, where the hit fractions, \V{r4b.above.covmin}--\V{r4b.above.covmax}\%, exceed the prespecified 97\% cutoff. No cell is below 90\% (reference ranges \V{v6.R4p.w2.lowd.rgmin}--\V{v6.R4p.w2.lowd.rgmax}\%). We do not call R4$'$ calibrated on this window. At $d=7$ the verdicts are mostly undecided. By the rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:metric}, \V{v7.w1.R4p.d7.undecided} of the 4 cells on the first window and \V{v7.w2.R4p.d7.undecided} on the second are undecided, and one cell per window ($p=10^{-3}$ on the first, $10^{-6}$ on the second) stays at or above 90\% over the whole reference range; none lies wholly inside 93--97\%. Coverage at the reference point estimates is \V{am.cov.pri.R4p.d7.t5}--\V{am.cov.pri.R4p.d7.t3}\% on the first window and \V{r4b.d7.covmin}--\V{r4b.d7.covmax}\% on the second, with reference ranges reaching down to \V{v6.R4p.w1.d7.rgmin}\% and \V{v6.R4p.w2.d7.rgmin}\%.

R4$'$ is not a recommendation: it was chosen after the fact, and the right exponent is not sufficient, since R2 has it and fails. A variant with the exponent $d/2$ (R4), the form we first registered and wrong for odd $d$, over-predicts by \V{ratio.R4.min} to \V{ratio.R4.max} times on the first window (\SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:r4}).

\subsection{Projection in distance}\label{sec:r5}
R5, which projects the R1 fits at $d=3$ and $5$ to $d=7$, fails by the largest factors in the study on the first window; its block-rate normalisation, an error of ours, overstates the $d=7$ rate by $\V{v6.alg}=\V{v6.algval}$ in the small-rate limit, and the per-round version is still \V{v6.r5.w1.rat.pr.t3} to \V{v6.r5.w1.rat.pr.t6} times the reference (\SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:r4}).

\section{Conclusions and limitations}
\looseness=-1 For a rotated surface-code memory at $d=3,5,7$ under uniform circuit-level depolarising noise, extrapolated in $p$ to $10^{-3}$--$10^{-6}$ and scored against independent reference estimates, nominal 95\% intervals from fits with the exponent fixed at $(d+1)/2$ and a constant prefactor showed low empirical coverage against the reference estimates on both windows, although their point predictions were close. That statement is only as good as the references: at the lowest rates they rest on two seeds of one estimator, whose common bias is not quantified, and on the assumption that strata with $w<(d+1)/2$ faults contain no failures. A free-exponent power law was far off on a window straddling the finite-size crossing and, with very wide intervals, covered on a window below it, where we had predicted severe undercoverage to persist. The leading power with a smooth prefactor covered about 95\% at $d=3$ and $5$ on the first window, post hoc, and missed its registered bar on the second (three of eight hit fractions exceeded the prespecified 97\% cutoff). We suggest, without having established a remedy, that the binomial error of a fixed-form fit not be read as the error of an extrapolation; that the fitted local slope across the window be reported beside $(d+1)/2$; that the leading power be checked against the smallest number of faults that defeats the decoder actually used; and that a rate be stated as per memory experiment or per round before $\Lambda$ is formed.

\looseness=-1 \emph{Limitations.} One code family, noise model and decoder, memory experiments with $d\le7$, two windows of five points each (their comparison is confounded with span and with distance from the targets), and our plain implementations of common forms; no published estimate was examined, and extrapolation in $d$ at fixed $p$ is touched only by R5. Direct Monte Carlo checks of the real-circuit reference estimates reached $\pL\approx\V{val.pmin}$; in a separate surrogate check, all 48 betting intervals at $d=3$ contained the bracket for the full surrogate value. The standard errors of the reference estimates are working ones, and the same twelve values score both windows. For the one real-circuit cell with a betting interval, $d=9$ at $p=10^{-4}$, the 95\% interval is $\V{c9.L}$ to $\V{c9.U}$ under the unproven assumption A-0 (no failure with four or fewer faults) and $\V{c9.free.L}$ to $\V{c9.free.U}$ without A-0 (\SM\ Sec.~\ref{S-sec:cert}). No remedy is established: R4$'$ missed its registered bar and two registered repairs failed. The methods of Refs.~\cite{bravyivargo2013,heussen2024,beverland2025,ye2026,molavi2026,mayer2025} are not compared, in particular not at equal computing cost.
