Resource estimates for fault-tolerant quantum computers rest on logical error rates p_L too small to simulate; they come from extrapolating fits made at high physical error rate p. We measure how often the 95% intervals of such fits contain reference estimates obtained independently by importance sampling stratified by the number of faults, for a rotated surface-code memory at d=3,5,7 under circuit-level depolarising noise with matching decoding, at p=10^-3 to 10^-6, with 300 replicates and two fit windows. Fits with the exponent fixed at (d+1)/2 and a constant prefactor have median predictions within 27% of the reference on the first window (p=4x10^-3 to 1.5x10^-2) and 21% on the second (1.5x10^-3 to 4x10^-3), but their upper 95% limits are only 2.8--5.1% above the prediction (R2, a constant per distance: 5.0--5.1%; R3, one shared curve: 2.8--4.6%). Coverage at the reference point estimates is below 90% in 24 of 24 cells on the first window and 19 of 24 on the second; observed hit fractions stayed below 90% throughout the stated reference ranges in 19 and 11 cells, and in none at or above it. These ranges do not cover a bias shared by the reference estimates or the assumed zero-failure strata. A free-exponent power law was high by up to 80-fold on the first window and covered 91.0--94.0% on the second, contrary to our prediction; the windows differ in span and distance as well as position. A form with the same leading power and a smooth prefactor covered 94.7--95.7% at d=3 and 5 on the first window (post hoc); on the second it missed its registered bar, three of eight hit fractions exceeding the prespecified 97% cutoff. At d=7 several verdicts are undecided (reference errors 7--8% at p<=10^-4). One code, noise model and decoder; no published estimate examined.
